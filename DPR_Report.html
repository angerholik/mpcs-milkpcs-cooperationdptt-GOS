1. Executive Summary

The Cooperative Oversight & Reporting Engine (CORe) is a flagship digital governance initiative formulated by the Department of Cooperation, Government of Sikkim. The primary mandate of this project is to technologically overhaul the auditing, monitoring, and compliance mechanisms of Milk Producers' Cooperative Societies (MPCS) operating across the state's diverse topographical districts.

By replacing archaic paper-based ledgers with a cryptographically secure, GPS-locked mobile reporting application combined with a centralized cloud-based web dashboard, the government will gain unprecedented real-time visibility into cooperative operations. This transparent framework aims to eliminate reporting redundancies, prevent fund misappropriation, ensure fair subsidy distribution, and elevate the socioeconomic standing of cooperative members.

2. Project Background & Rationale

2.1 The Current Scenario

Sikkim boasts a robust network of Milk Producers' Cooperative Societies that serve as the economic backbone for rural dairy farmers. Currently, the reporting mechanism heavily relies on manual data entry by field extension officers. Supervisors physically travel to collection centers, manually record milk yields, demographic participation (SC/ST/OBC/Gen), and financial liabilities (loans/advances), and submit physical returns to the head office.

2.2 Identified Bottlenecks

  • Data Latency: Information propagation from rural centers to the central directory takes weeks, severely crippling timely decision-making and subsidy disbursement.
  • Absence of Spatial Verification: Physical inspections cannot be definitively proven. Supervisors may submit reports without actually visiting the MPCS.
  • Data Integrity Risks: Manual aggregation of financial metrics (ledgers, bank balances, loan recoveries) often leads to arithmetic discrepancies, masking potential systemic inefficiencies or fund leakages.
  • Storage & Retrieval: Retrieving historical compliance data for statutory audits is highly labor-intensive and prone to document degradation.

3. Core Objectives & Scope of Work

The CORe project is bounded by the following strategic objectives:

  1. Real-Time Data Acquisition: Deploy an offline-first mobile application (CORe Field App) for extension officers to capture physical evidence, digital ledgers, and demographic data.
  2. Geospatial Enforceability: Mandate GPS locking. Actionable reports can only be generated if the device coordinates match the surveyed geo-fenced perimeters of the cooperative center.
  3. Automated Compliance Documentation: Upon completion of a field survey, the system must instantly generate a Government-branded, A4-sized PDF Report strictly formatted to departmental standards, acting as an immutable Return Certificate.
  4. Executive Command Center: Implement a high-level centralized dashboard (CORe Command) for the Secretariat to view state-wide aggregates, drill down to block/panchayat levels, and issue systemic broadcasts.

5.1 CORe Field (Mobile Component)

Targeted at ARCS (Assistant Registrar of Cooperative Societies) and Cooperative Inspectors. Core modules include:

  • Digital Evidence Vault: Utilizes device camera with mandated location permissions. Photos are compressed, EXIF tagged, and stored.
  • Institutional Ledger Matrix: UI captures milk collection stats (Litres), total withdrawals, and current bank balance. Discrepancies prompt hard-stops.
  • Demographic Census Widget: Dynamic calculation of Male/Female participants categorized by social strata (SC/ST/OBC/GEN) to align with state inclusive policies.
  • Liability & Debt Control: Tracks scheme loans against individual centers, highlighting active arrears and warning thresholds.

5.2 CORe Command (Web Secretariat Portal)

Targeted at the Secretary, Registrar, and Joint Registrars. Modules include:

  • Geospatial Intelligence Map: Plotting all registered MPCS on the state map using coordinates fetched from field reports.
  • Real-time KPI Metrics: Live tracking of total milk collected state-wide, outstanding loan recoveries, and active inspections.
  • Headquarters Broadcast Directive: Ability to push instant urgent notifications down to all field devices seamlessly.

6. Technological Stack & Security Framework

The system utilizes a highly scalable, modern, edge-computed stack ensuring longevity, rapid iteration, and zero vendor-lock-in vulnerabilities.

Frontend Field App (Mobile)

React Native & Expo: Allows cross-platform deployment (Android/iOS). Utilizes core Native APIs for Camera, GPS caching, and Expo-Print for HTML-to-PDF generation.

Frontend Command (Web)

React JS & Vite: Lightning-fast web rendering. Data visualization powered by Recharts and cartography powered by Leaflet.js.

Cloud & Database Infrastructure

Supabase (PostgreSQL): Acts as the secure Backend-as-a-Service (BaaS). Provides high-performance Relational Database mapping, Row-Level Security (RLS), and secure Edge Functions.

Security & Authentication

JWT Token Authentication: Stateless, highly secure token systems. Cryptographic monitoring ensures no unauthenticated payloads reach the central database.

Data Sovereignty Standard: All data transmissions are encrypted via TLS 1.3 protocol. Access controls are rigorously enforced via Supabase Row-Level Policies ensuring Officers only see the districts assigned to their jurisdiction.

7. Implementation Strategy & Target Milestones

The project will be executed in three rapid, agile phases spanning 6 months:

  • Phase I: Infrastructure Formulation (Months 1-2): Core application development, database architecture, and integration of the PDF generator engine (Currently Active).
  • Phase II: Beta Pilot & Training (Months 3-4): Deployment of mobile applications to 50 pilot officers in the Gangtok and Namchi districts. Real-world stress testing of the offline-sync logic.
  • Phase III: State-wide Go-Live (Months 5-6): Full transition from paper. Decommissioning of old ledgers. Deployment of the Web Dashboard to the Secretariat.

9. Expected Impact & Return on Investment (ROI)

The successful deployment of CORe guarantees a profound qualitative shift in governance:

  • Erratic Claims Mitigation: Direct photographic and GPS evidence strictly halts phantom reports and ensures subsidies are disbursed to active, physically verified societies.
  • Zero-Latency Policy Making: The Secretary of Cooperation gains real-time data, enabling rapid policy interventions rather than acting on months-old lag metrics.
  • Eco-Friendly Operations: Substantial reduction in physical paper trails, transportation logistics, and document physical archiving costs.
  • Institutional Accountability: Establishes a permanent, immutable digital trail of all financial reporting activities conducted by department personnel.
